SECTION A: ANALYZE — DEVIATIONS IDENTIFIED IN THE ORIGINAL GDD

A careful comparison of the original Game_Play.txt against the core concept
revealed 24 specific deviations. These are grouped by category below.

          The core concept requires this as the first screen after the title.

          both representing Paap/Obstacles.
  [A-03] Support items: GDD lists only "TTC Bus." Core concept specifies

          Mobile, Food, and Movie Ticket as the three distraction types.

          Core concept says monk should also identify the Personal Car box.
  [A-06] Transition: GDD describes an "animation of removing shoes in front
          of the temple." Core concept specifies a Background Image of the
          actual JSOT Temple with the character walking toward it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION B: EVALUATE — CRITIQUE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What the GDD gets RIGHT (retained in the refined version):
  (+) Title "The Path to Shikhar" — evocative, spiritually resonant.
  (+) Core Philosophy "Guidance over Gating" — perfectly captures the monk
      mechanic's intent and reward structure.
  (+) Level thematic names: The Commute (Samsara), The Cave (Resilience),
      The Hall (Valor), The Summit (Moksha) — each name reflects both the
      physical setting and a spiritual state the player must traverse.
  (+) Level settings (Toronto cityscape, stone cave, marble hall, mountain
      summit) — rich visual context that adds meaning without contradiction.
  (+) Monk placement locations (bus stop, cave alcove, marble lotus, peak
      ledge) — imaginative and contextually appropriate.
  (+) Spiritual questions — well-researched, correct, and thematically tied
      to each Bhagwan. Retained as-is.
  (+) Victory screen showing Digambar Garbhalaya — matches core concept.
  (+) Box Roulette structure (1 Goal, 2 Support, 3 Distraction) — a clean
      and playable distribution for 6 boxes. Retained and extended with the
      no-effect Wrong Lakshan category as needed.

What the GDD gets WRONG (corrected in the refined version):
  (-) The Samagri items (Jal in L2, Chandan in L3) appear to be the author's
      own creative interpretation. While thematically interesting (Jal ties
      to Parshvanath's serpent-shield story; Chandan to Mahavir's puja), they
      contradict the core concept, which specifies Akshat uniformly. The core
      concept must take precedence.
  (-) Fire is dropped from L1, L2, and L4 entirely — a significant omission
      since Fire alongside Water is the core hazard metaphor throughout.
  (-) Support items are consistently underdefined. Ghanta and Chanvar are
      important Jain ritual objects whose inclusion was specifically requested
      and carry cultural meaning.
  (-) The Wrong Lakshan / no-effect box is a nuanced and well-designed mechanic
      the GDD ignores completely, flattening what should be a three-tier box
      system (Goal / Support / Distraction / No-Effect) per level.
  (-) All four transitions omit the Bhagwan images that are central to the
      spiritual experience the game is trying to create.
  (-) The scoring system overcomplicates the user's clear, elegant directive:
      the winner is the fastest devotee.
  (-) The named rankings (Moksha Margi, Shravak, Bhakt) are creative but
      were not requested. They have been retained as an OPTIONAL enhancement,
      clearly labelled as such, rather than elevated to core mechanics.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION C: REFINED APPROACH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following principles guided all corrections:

  1. Core concept is authoritative. Where GDD and core concept conflict,
     the core concept wins without exception.
  2. Good GDD additions are retained. Thematic naming, spiritual questions,
     and setting descriptions are preserved — they strengthen the document
     without contradicting anything.
  3. Consistency is enforced. Water AND Fire appear as hazards in all four
     levels. Akshat is the goal Samagri across Levels 2, 3, and 4.
  4. The no-effect box mechanic is fully documented for all levels.
  5. Scoring is simplified to match the core concept. Rankings are noted
     as optional.
  6. All transitions are corrected to include both the Bhagwan image and
     the animation.

II. CORE SYSTEMS & MECHANICS
================================================================================

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE MONK / GUIDE SYSTEM ("Choice of Guidance")
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Role:
    The Monk is a passive NPC placed in a safe, reachable area of each level.

  Interaction:
    Completely optional. The player may walk past him at any time.
    Trigger: Press UP arrow near the Monk.
    Result: Game pauses. Dialogue window opens with a spiritual question.

  The Wager:
    Correct Answer:
      The camera pans to reveal the specific box containing the level's Goal
      Item (Key or Samagri) and/or the primary Support item. The player saves
      significant search time.
    Wrong Answer or Skip:
      Player must manually open boxes, risking Distraction penalties and
      wasted time.

  Note: The Monk cannot be harmed and poses no threat. He is a guide, not
  a gatekeeper. Players who ignore him are not punished — only those who
  interact and answer wrongly lose the guidance advantage.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE BOX SYSTEM ("Box Roulette")
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Every level contains 5 to 6 identical, visually indistinguishable boxes.
  Their contents are randomly shuffled at the start of each play session.

  BOX CONTENT CATEGORIES:

  Category 1 — GOAL (1 box per level):
    The required item to complete and exit the level.
    Level 1: Temple Key
    Level 2: Samagri (Akshat)
    Level 3: Samagri (Akshat)
    Level 4: Samagri (Akshat)

  Category 2 — SUPPORT (1–2 boxes per level):
    Beneficial items. Each support item ADDS time to the countdown clock.
    Support items vary by level (see Level Architecture below).

  Category 3 — DISTRACTION (2–3 boxes per level):
    Penalty items. Each distraction item SUBTRACTS time from the countdown
    clock and briefly stuns or freezes the player character.
    Common distractions across levels: Mobile Phone, Friend, Foe.
    Level 1 specific: Mobile Phone, Food, Movie Ticket.

  Category 4 — NO EFFECT (1 box in Levels 2, 3, and 4):
    A "Wrong Lakshan" — an item from a different Bhagwan's symbolic set
    that does not apply here. Opening it has no positive or negative effect.
    The player simply moves on. This teaches discernment.
    Level 2 no-effect: Wrong Lakshan (Bull)
    Level 3 no-effect: Wrong Lakshan (Bull)
    Level 4 no-effect: Wrong Lakshan (Lion)

  HAZARD SYSTEM (Universal Across All Levels):
    Water Hazard: Represents attachment and the pull of Samsara.
      Contact causes a time penalty or character stun.
    Fire Hazard: Represents Kashaya (passions) — anger, ego, greed, deceit.
      Contact causes a time penalty or character stun.
    Both Water and Fire hazards appear in every level. Their visual form
    and intensity scale with level difficulty.

III. LEVEL ARCHITECTURE (THE CLIMB)
================================================================================

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LEVEL 1: THE COMMUTE  (Samsara)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Setting:
    Toronto Cityscape — Pixel Art.
    Grey pavement, subway tiles, brick houses, TTC signage, storefronts.

  Objective:
    Navigate from the Home Gate to the Temple Gate.

  Goal Item:
    Temple Key (hidden in one of the boxes).

  Hazards:
    Water: Construction pits filled with water. Puddles on the road.
    Fire:  Exposed gas flames from construction sites. Hot steam vents.
    Other: Traffic cones narrowing the path. Moving vehicles on the road.

  Platforming Difficulty:
    Easy. Static platforms. Simple horizontal gaps. Forgiving jump arcs.
    Suitable for players learning the controls.

  The Guide (Monk):
    Location: Seated at a Bus Stop bench, reading.
    Question: "To find peace, what must one leave behind?"
      Choice A: Ego   [Correct]
      Choice B: Car   [Incorrect]
    Reward: Camera pans to reveal the box containing the Temple Key
            and/or the Personal Car support box.

  Box Contents (6 boxes total):
    Goal (1x):        Temple Key — required to exit to Level 2.
    Support (2x):     TTC Bus — adds time; grants brief speed boost.
                      Personal Car — adds more time; faster boost.
    Distraction (3x): Mobile Phone — player stops to check screen (time lost).
                      Food — player stops to eat (time lost).
                      Movie Ticket — player daydreams (time lost).

  Transition to Level 2:
    Screen: Full background image of the Jain Society of Toronto (JSOT)
            temple exterior.
    Animation: Player character walks toward and through the temple entrance,
               pausing momentarily at the threshold before the screen fades.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LEVEL 2: THE CAVE  (Resilience)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Setting:
    Darker, stone-textured passage leading through the temple's lower section
    toward the Parshvanath Bhagwan Garbhalaya.

  Objective:
    Navigate from the Temple Gate to the Parshvanath Bhagwan Garbhalaya Gate.

  Goal Item:
    Samagri (Akshat — sacred rice) hidden in one of the boxes.

  Hazards:
    Water: Rain shafts and waterfalls (Upsarg — the trial of Kamatha).
           Contact stuns the player and drains time.
    Fire:  Torch sconces that flare unexpectedly. Ember showers from above.
    Note:  The Lakshan (Snake) support item grants temporary immunity to
           the Water hazard, mirroring Dharanendra's protection of the Lord.

  Platforming Difficulty:
    Medium. Moving stone platforms (floating in water channels). Wider gaps.
    Timing of jumps begins to matter.

  The Guide (Monk):
    Location: Inside a meditation alcove carved into the cave wall.
    Question: "Who shielded the Lord from the storm?"
      Choice A: Dharanendra   [Correct — the protective serpent deity]
      Choice B: Kamatha       [Incorrect — the source of the storm]
    Reward: Camera pans to reveal the box containing the Akshat
            and/or the primary Support box.

  Box Contents (5–6 boxes total):
    Goal (1x):        Samagri (Akshat) — required to exit to Level 3.
    Support (2x):     Ghanta (Temple Bell) — rings to repel hazards; adds time.
                      Lakshan (Snake) — grants Water hazard immunity; adds time.
    No Effect (1x):   Wrong Lakshan (Bull) — player picks it up; nothing happens.
    Distraction (2x): Mobile Phone — player takes a selfie; controls freeze briefly.
                      Friend — friend appears and chats; controls freeze briefly.
                      Foe — adversary taunts player; controls freeze briefly.
    (Distribute 2–3 Distraction boxes depending on total box count for the level.)

  Transition to Level 3:
    Screen: Full image of Parshvanath Bhagwan.
    Animation: Player character approaches the idol and bows reverently.
               Screen holds on the image for a moment before fading.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LEVEL 3: THE HALL  (Valor)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Setting:
    White marble and gold interior of the temple — bright, clean, reflective.
    The path to Mahavir Bhagwan's Garbhalaya is lined with pillars and light.

  Objective:
    Navigate from the Parshvanath Bhagwan Garbhalaya Gate to the
    Mahavir Bhagwan Garbhalaya Gate.

  Goal Item:
    Samagri (Akshat — sacred rice) hidden in one of the boxes.

  Hazards:
    Fire: Fire pits along the floor, representing Kashaya (inner passions —
          anger, pride, deceit, greed). Cannot be crossed without caution.
    Water: Reflecting pools that overflow, creating slippery water patches
           that slow the player and drain time on contact.
    Platform Challenge: Disappearing marble tiles ("Illusions of permanence").
           Tiles fade in and out. The player must memorise the safe path
           or use the Lakshan (Lion) support to reveal it temporarily.

  Platforming Difficulty:
    Hard. Disappearing platforms require pattern recognition or support use.
    Multiple hazard types active simultaneously.

  The Guide (Monk):
    Location: Seated on a floating marble lotus platform above the hall.
    Question: "What is the weapon of the Tirthankara?"
      Choice A: Ahimsa   [Correct — non-violence is the supreme force]
      Choice B: Sword    [Incorrect]
    Reward: Camera pans to reveal the box containing the Akshat
            and/or the primary Support box.

  Box Contents (5–6 boxes total):
    Goal (1x):        Samagri (Akshat) — required to exit to Level 4.
    Support (2x):     Chanvar (Ceremonial Fan) — clears fire hazards briefly;
                                                 adds time.
                      Lakshan (Lion) — reveals all invisible platforms for 10
                                       seconds; adds time.
    No Effect (1x):   Wrong Lakshan (Bull) — player picks it up; nothing happens.
    Distraction (2–3x): Mobile Phone — player checks notifications; freeze.
                         Friend — friend sprite appears and talks; freeze.
                         Foe — foe provokes the player; freeze.

  Transition to Level 4:
    Screen: Full image of Mahavir Bhagwan.
    Animation: Player character approaches the idol and bows reverently.
               Screen holds on the image for a moment before fading.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
LEVEL 4: THE SUMMIT  (Moksha)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Setting:
    The Shikhar — the temple's peak and final sanctum. White, gold, and open
    sky. The atmosphere is serene but the path is narrow and demanding.
    This is the culmination of the pilgrimage.

  Objective:
    Navigate from the Mahavir Bhagwan Garbhalaya Gate to the
    Moolnayak Adinath Bhagwan Garbhalaya Gate.

  Goal Item:
    Samagri (Akshat — sacred rice) hidden in one of the boxes.

  Hazards:
    Water: Sacred water channels along the summit path. Falling in costs time.
    Fire:  Ceremonial flame pillars flanking the path. Timing is essential.
    Platform Challenge: Crumbling stone blocks that begin to break 1 second
           after the player lands on them. Precision and speed are required.
           Small, narrow platforms with minimal margin for error.

  Platforming Difficulty:
    Expert. The hardest level. Every jump counts. Crumbling blocks punish
    hesitation. Multiple hazards overlap. This is the final test of focus.

  The Guide (Monk):
    Location: A high peak ledge just before the final approach to the idol.
    Question: "Who was the first to show the path?"
      Choice A: Adinath   [Correct — the first Tirthankara, the Moolnayak]
      Choice B: Bahubali  [Incorrect — Bahubali attained moksha but did not
                           show the path for others as a Tirthankara]
    Reward: Camera pans to reveal the box containing the Akshat
            and/or the primary Support box.

  Box Contents (5–6 boxes total):
    Goal (1x):        Samagri (Akshat) — required to complete the pilgrimage.
    Support (2x):     Ghanta (Temple Bell) — repels hazards; adds time.
                      Chanvar (Ceremonial Fan) — clears fire hazards; adds time.
                      Lakshan (Bull) — grants enhanced jump height to cross
                                       tall obstacles; adds time.
    No Effect (1x):   Wrong Lakshan (Lion) — player picks it up; nothing happens.
    Distraction (2x): Mobile Phone — player checks screen; controls freeze.
                      Friend — appears and converses; controls freeze.
                      Foe — provokes player; controls freeze.
    (At Level 4, aim for 3 Support boxes across Ghanta / Chanvar / Lakshan
     to give the player meaningful choice in the final stretch.)

  Transition to Victory:
    Screen: Full image of Moolnayak Adinath Bhagwan.
    Animation: Player character approaches the idol and bows in deep reverence.
               The screen holds on the image. A soft golden glow emanates.
               Screen fades into the Victory / Final Score page.

IV. VICTORY & SCORING
================================================================================

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FINAL SCREEN
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Visual: A high-quality image of the Digambar Garbhalaya is displayed.
  The player's score is shown beneath it.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SCORING
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Core Metric:
    Winning Score = Total time taken to complete all four levels.
    Lower time = higher rank. Speed of devotion is rewarded.

  OPTIONAL ENHANCEMENT (for future versions):
    The following bonus system may be layered on top of the time score
    to add strategic depth without changing the core time-based measure:

    Focus Bonus:   +500 points for every Distraction box left UNOPENED.
                   Rewards players who search wisely rather than randomly.
    Wisdom Bonus:  +1000 points for every Monk question answered correctly.
                   Rewards spiritual knowledge alongside platform skill.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RANKINGS  (Optional — for leaderboard display)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  These titles may be displayed alongside the score for flavour:

    Fastest completion time:  Moksha Margi  (One on the path to liberation)
    Mid-range completion:     Shravak       (A devoted lay follower)
    Slowest completion:       Bhakt         (A sincere but learning devotee)

  Note: All three titles carry respect. No player is shamed. The ranking
  reflects speed of the pilgrimage, not worthiness of the devotee.
